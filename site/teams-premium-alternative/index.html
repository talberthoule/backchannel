Why switch

Why people look for a Teams Premium alternative

Microsoft builds good meeting AI. The reasons people go looking elsewhere are about licensing and scope, not quality.

Recap is behind a paywall

Microsoft's own licensing documentation puts Intelligent Meeting Recap, AI-generated notes and tasks, recap for meetings you missed, and speaker timeline markers in Teams Premium, not base Teams. As of July 2026 that is $10.00 per user per month paid yearly, or a Microsoft 365 Copilot license instead.

Almost nobody actually has the license

Microsoft reported more than 20 million paid Microsoft 365 Copilot seats in its FY26 Q3 results in April 2026, against roughly 450 million Microsoft 365 commercial seats reported that January. That is under 5 percent. For everyone else, the choice is not Backchannel versus Copilot -- it is Backchannel versus nothing.

It only covers Teams meetings

Teams recap works on Teams calls. If you also sit in customers' Zoom rooms, partners' Google Meet calls, a Webex bridge, or a phone call on speaker, your employer's Teams investment covers none of it. One assistant per platform is three assistants.

IT can withhold it or switch it off

The license is the first gate and the biggest one. Beyond it, many Teams Premium features need admin configuration before anyone can use them, meeting AI can be locked off at tenant level, and from July 2026 organizers and presenters can disable Copilot, Facilitator, and recap during a live meeting.

The alternative

Backchannel: your own meeting assistant, on your own hardware

Backchannel is an open-source AI meeting assistant you run yourself, as a desktop build for Windows, macOS, and Linux or with Docker Compose. It captures your microphone and, optionally, tab or system audio directly in the browser -- no bot joins the meeting, so it works with Teams, Zoom, Google Meet, Webex, or any app at all. Voice activity detection and speaker diarization run locally, building a live speaker-attributed transcript as you talk.

While the call is still happening, a crew of nine seeded agents works on that transcript. An audio gateway produces interim transcription. Five live insight agents run on their own schedules: a consolidated analyst every 40 seconds for questions, observations, opportunities, and action items; an objection handler every 10 seconds over only the last 90 seconds of speech, pairing an immediate suggested response with the underlying concern; a synthesizer that reconciles and elevates saved insights; an opportunity specialist that matches opportunities against your own offerings catalog; and a strategic signals agent every 45 seconds producing Signal, Risk, Next Question, Opportunity, and Action Cue cards. When the call ends, a three-agent briefing pass drafts, cross-checks, and settles the written record. Every agent's model, prompt, and trigger is editable, and any of them can be turned off per session.

The trade is real. You give up Microsoft's exact speaker identity, its roughly 25-language recap, its ability to summarize meetings you never attended, and the whole enterprise compliance apparatus. In exchange, recordings and transcripts stay on your disk, nothing is metered or licensed per seat, the same tool covers every platform you join, and the entire pipeline is MIT-licensed and auditable.

The differences that actually matter

Who actually holds a license

This is the whole argument, and it is arithmetic rather than opinion. Microsoft reported more than 20 million paid Microsoft 365 Copilot seats in April 2026, against roughly 450 million Microsoft 365 commercial seats reported that January. Under 5 percent. Add the people whose tenant bought Teams Premium but never configured the features, and the people whose admin locked meeting AI off, and the realistic picture is that the large majority of daily Teams users have no AI meeting assistance at all. Those people are not choosing between two products. They are choosing between something and nothing.

One assistant, not one per platform

We will not overclaim here, because the ground moved this year. Google's "take notes for me" now covers in-person and on-the-go meetings and, per Google's Cloud Next announcement in April 2026, meetings "hosted on another provider like Zoom or Teams". Zoom's assistant can join Google Meet and Microsoft Teams meetings, though it does so as a visible participant whose tile carries Zoom branding and a "Transcribing" status. So cross-platform capture is not uniquely ours. What is ours is the conjunction: no bot in anyone's meeting, no vendor cloud, live tactical assistance, and no per-seat license -- all four at once. Microsoft, meanwhile, offers no cross-platform story at all.

Assistance during the call, not a document after it

Every bundled assistant in this category is reactive. Copilot answers what you type into a side pane. Zoom answers preset or custom questions once the host starts the feature. Ask Gemini answers what you ask it. None of them volunteers that the prospect just raised a pricing objection and here is a response, or that something they said maps to a service you sell. Backchannel's objection handler runs every 10 seconds over the last 90 seconds of speech and pushes a suggested response with the concern behind it; the strategic signals agent posts Signal, Risk, Next Question, Opportunity, and Action Cue cards every 45 seconds. If your meetings are discovery or sales calls, a prompt you can act on beats a summary you read later.

Where the audio lives

All three bundled assistants process meeting audio in their vendor's cloud. Each offers regional controls -- Google lets admins pin covered Workspace data to the US or EU, Zoom offers regional processing options for non-US customers -- and each carries certifications Backchannel does not have. Backchannel's story is simpler rather than stronger on paper: audio is captured in your browser, diarized on your machine, and stored on your disk. With a local ONNX transcription model even transcription happens offline, and the text-analysis agents route to a provider you choose -- or to a self-hosted OpenAI-compatible server on your own machine or LAN, so a fully local deployment calls out to nobody. Details are in the audio pipeline docs.

What we are worse at

Microsoft knows precisely who is speaking because it owns the participant list. Backchannel infers speakers from voice embeddings and will sometimes merge two people or split one. Microsoft's recap spans roughly 25 languages and its live translated captions span 40; Backchannel documents no language support and is English-first in practice. Teams can recap a meeting you never joined, which device-audio capture structurally cannot do. And Backchannel has no SSO, no DLP, no eDiscovery, no retention policy, and no user-authentication boundary -- it will not pass an enterprise security review, and we are not going to pretend otherwise.

Who should use which

Stay with Microsoft, Zoom, or Google if you...

Already hold the license and mainly want post-call recap; run meetings in languages other than English; need to recap meetings you did not attend; capture in-person meetings from a phone; or buy through a procurement process that requires SSO, DLP, eDiscovery, and retention controls. In all of those cases the bundled assistant is the better tool and costs you nothing extra.

Add Backchannel if you...

Use Teams every day but your tenant never bought Premium or Copilot; join customer calls across Zoom, Meet, Webex, and Teams and want one assistant for all of them; want objection responses, opportunity flags, and questions worth asking pushed to you mid-call rather than a recap afterwards; or work under a policy where no vendor-cloud notetaker is permitted at all.

What getting started looks like

There is no tenant to configure and no admin to ask. Download a desktop build for Windows, macOS, or Linux, or clone the repo and run docker compose up --build. Add a Gemini or OpenAI API key -- or run the whole stack locally: ONNX transcription, a self-hosted model for the agents, and the on-device captioner, with Privacy First on. The quickstart is honest about the friction: the first start builds images and downloads models, so give it a few minutes.

Past meetings: Backchannel imports transcripts (.txt, .md, .docx) and audio files (.mp3, .m4a, .wav, .ogg, .flac), and can run the same analysis over an imported transcript. There is no export bridge from Teams, Zoom, or Google Drive -- you export from them and import the files you care about.

One thing worth setting up early: if you sell or consult, load your offerings catalog. The opportunity specialist matches insights from live calls against it, which is the piece none of the bundled assistants can do because none of them knows what you sell.

Common questions

Do I need Teams Premium or Microsoft 365 Copilot to get AI meeting recap in Teams?

Yes. As of July 2026, Microsoft's licensing documentation puts Intelligent Meeting Recap, AI-generated notes and tasks, recap for meetings you missed, speaker timeline markers, and multilingual meeting support in Teams Premium, not in base Teams. Those recap experiences are also available with a Microsoft 365 Copilot license. Base Teams gets transcripts and recordings, not AI recap.

If my company already pays for Teams Premium or Copilot, is there any reason to add Backchannel?

Often no, and we would rather say so. If you have the license and you want post-call recap of Teams meetings, Microsoft already does that with better speaker accuracy, no setup, and your compliance team's approval. Backchannel is worth adding only for three specific needs: one assistant across every meeting app instead of one, tactical prompts pushed to you during the call rather than a summary after it, or audio and transcripts that never leave hardware you control.

Does Backchannel work with Zoom, Google Meet, and Teams?

Yes, with all of them, because it never joins the call. Backchannel captures your microphone and, optionally, tab or system audio directly in the browser, so there is no bot participant and no per-platform integration. It works the same way on a platform that has no AI features at all, on a recorded call playing in a browser tab, and on a phone call on speaker.

What about Zoom AI Companion and Google Meet's take notes for me?

Both are genuinely bundled and both now reach past their own platform, so we will not pretend cross-platform is ours alone. Zoom's assistant can join Google Meet and Microsoft Teams meetings as a visible participant that shows Zoom branding and a Transcribing status. Google's take notes for me covers in-person and on-the-go meetings and, per Google's Cloud Next announcement in April 2026, meetings hosted on another provider like Zoom or Teams. What neither does is push tactical suggestions at you during the call, and neither keeps the audio on your own hardware.

What do Teams Premium and Microsoft 365 Copilot do better than Backchannel?

Several things, and they matter. Microsoft knows exactly who is speaking because it owns the participant list, while Backchannel infers speakers acoustically and will sometimes be wrong. Microsoft's recap covers roughly 25 languages; Backchannel documents none and is English-first in practice. Microsoft can recap a meeting you never attended, which device-audio capture cannot do. And Microsoft brings SSO, DLP, eDiscovery, retention, and sensitivity labels; Backchannel has no user-authentication boundary at all.

What does running Backchannel actually involve?

Either download a desktop build for Windows, macOS, or Linux, or run docker compose up --build and open the browser app. You add a Gemini or OpenAI API key -- or run the whole stack locally: ONNX transcription, a self-hosted model for the agents, and the on-device captioner, with Privacy First on. Speaker diarization always runs locally. There are no seats to buy, no minutes to meter, and no tenant to configure, because the whole thing runs on your own machine.
