An open-source, self-hosted Fathom alternative

Let us start by conceding the obvious: Fathom's free plan is genuinely excellent. Unlimited recordings, unlimited storage, and unlimited transcription in 38 languages, forever, with zero setup. Nothing on this page will tell you Backchannel is cheaper or easier to start than that, because it is not. What Backchannel offers instead is different ownership and a different kind of help: your meetings never reach a vendor, and agents work the call while it is still running.

The honest starting point

Where the free plan actually ends

Fathom's free tier is the strongest in the category. It has exactly one meaningful cap, and it is worth knowing precisely where it sits. Plan details as of July 2026.

What really is unlimited

Fathom's help center states that Free and Premium both include unlimited recordings and storage, and unlimited transcriptions in 38 languages. This is not a trial or a teaser. For a solo user who wants a searchable record of every call, Fathom free is a genuinely good answer.

The one real cap

Advanced summaries on Free are limited to "your first 5 calls each month"; after that only general templates are available. AI action items, AI follow-up emails, custom summaries, and Ask Fathom are Premium-only, at $20 per user per month, or $16 annually.

Where cost does start to matter

The free plan is an individual story. Team is $19 per user monthly or $15 annually, and Business is $34 monthly or $25 annually. At twenty seats on Business, that is roughly $6,000 a year for a capability Backchannel includes at $0 beyond your own hardware and API usage.

What the free plan costs in another currency

Fathom's privacy policy says that, "Based on how you configure your account settings, we may use and create de-identified data generated from Meeting Content Information to improve our Services by training, improving, and customizing our in-house artificial intelligence models." You can opt out in account settings -- but it is an opt-out, not an opt-in.

The alternative

The trade is ownership, not price

Backchannel is an MIT-licensed AI meeting assistant you run yourself with Docker Compose, with desktop builds for Windows, macOS, and Linux. It captures your microphone and, optionally, tab or system audio directly in the browser -- no bot joins the meeting, on any platform, so it works with Zoom, Google Meet, Teams, or anything else. Voice activity detection and speaker diarization run locally on every call, and transcription can run fully offline with local ONNX Whisper or Parakeet models.

Fathom is a cloud service, and its privacy policy is candid about what that means. Its services are hosted in the United States, and de-identified meeting content may be used to train Fathom's in-house models depending on your account settings. To Fathom's credit, the same policy states plainly that it does not authorize third parties such as OpenAI, Anthropic, or Google to train their models on your meeting content. But the structural fact remains: your conversations are on someone else's infrastructure, under terms they set and can revise.

With Backchannel there is no vendor in that position. Audio is captured in your browser, diarized on your server, and stored on your disk. With a local transcription model, even the speech-to-text runs offline. The only outbound calls are the ones you configure to the model provider whose key you supply.

The differences that actually matter

Live summaries versus live advice

Fathom's 2026 desktop update added live summaries that update as the call unfolds, plus a scratchpad for your own notes. That is real and it is useful. But it is a better record of what already happened. Backchannel's agents are pointed the other way: a consolidated analyst on a 40-second cycle proposing questions and observations, an objection handler running every 10 seconds over just the last 90 seconds of conversation and pairing an immediate suggested response with the underlying concern, an opportunity specialist matching what you heard against your own offerings catalog, a synthesizer reconciling insights and noticing when a question has been answered, and a strategic signals agent producing risk and next-question cards every 45 seconds. Fathom's own roadmap lists real-time coaching as coming soon, which suggests they agree this is where the category is going.

Bot-free is no longer the differentiator

We should be straight about this, because a lot of comparison pages are not. Fathom added bot-free capture and it works well. The remaining gap is narrow and platform-shaped: as of July 2026 it is Mac only, with Windows still listed as coming soon, whereas Backchannel's browser capture has never involved a bot on any operating system. If you are on a Mac and the bot was your only complaint, Fathom already solved your problem.

What you give up

Honestly: quite a lot. Fathom is used at 300,000-plus companies and has SOC 2 Type II, GDPR, and HIPAA compliance with SSO and SCIM -- badges a procurement team understands and Backchannel simply does not have. Fathom does 38 languages; we market no language matrix at all. Fathom syncs to HubSpot, Salesforce, and Close, has clips, playlists, and search across every call, and integrates with ChatGPT and Claude. Backchannel offers exports, a cross-session chat, and a REST API. Fathom installs in a minute; Backchannel is a Docker Compose stack, and our prebuilt desktop downloads need an approved Backchannel account rather than being a direct download.

One rough edge worth naming

Backchannel's analysis agents can now run against a self-hosted OpenAI-compatible server (Ollama, LM Studio, vLLM), so with local ONNX transcription the whole pipeline works with the network off and no key from anyone. The wrinkle is that our Privacy First switch does not yet know that: it recognizes only the local transcription models, so switching it on still disables every outside call, including the agents. If you need summarization with zero cloud calls, look at the other open-source options -- some of them do that better than we do.

Who should use which

Stay with Fathom if you...

Are a solo user or small team who wants a great free record of every call with no setup; need 38-language coverage, CRM sync, or compliance certifications; work on a Mac where bot-free capture already ships; and are comfortable with cloud processing under a US-hosted service.

Switch to Backchannel if you...

Cannot have meeting audio on a vendor's infrastructure at any price, do not want an opt-out training arrangement, want tactical suggestions during the call rather than a live summary of it, want to edit the agents' prompts and models yourself, and can run docker compose with your own API key.

What moving over looks like

Setup is a git clone and docker compose up --build, plus a Gemini or OpenAI API key (or a local ONNX Whisper/Parakeet model for fully offline transcription). The quickstart is honest about the friction: first start builds images and downloads models, so give it a few minutes, not seconds. Compared with installing Fathom, this is the harder path and we are not going to dress that up.

Past meetings: Backchannel imports transcripts (.txt, .md, .docx) and audio files (.mp3, .m4a, .wav, .ogg, .flac), and can run its analysis over imported content. Because raw audio is retained, a session can also be re-transcribed later through a different model. There is no one-click Fathom migration tool -- you export from Fathom and import what you care about.

Common questions

Is Fathom's free plan really unlimited?

For recording and transcription, yes, and we will not pretend otherwise. As of July 2026 Fathom's help center states that both Free and Premium include unlimited recordings and storage plus unlimited transcriptions in 38 languages. The one real cap on Free is AI summarization: advanced summaries are limited to your first 5 calls each month, after which only general templates are available. AI action items, follow-up emails, custom summaries, and Ask Fathom are Premium features.

Why switch from Fathom if it is free?

Only two reasons are good enough. The first is where your meetings live: Fathom's privacy policy states that, depending on your account settings, it may use de-identified data from your meeting content to train and improve its in-house AI models, with opt-out available in account settings, and its services are hosted in the United States. Backchannel processes and stores everything on hardware you control. The second is what you get during the call: Fathom's live layer describes what is happening, while Backchannel's agents suggest what to do next.

Does Fathom still put a bot in your meeting?

Not always any more. Fathom now offers a choice of bot-free or bot capture, which is a genuine improvement and removes what had been its most common complaint. As of July 2026, however, bot-free capture is available on Mac only, with Windows listed as coming soon. Backchannel's browser capture has never used a bot, on any platform, since nothing ever joins the call.

What does Fathom do better than Backchannel?

A great deal. Fathom offers zero-setup onboarding, unlimited transcription in 38 languages, a polished native desktop app, CRM sync with HubSpot, Salesforce, and Close, clips, playlists, search across every call, ChatGPT and Claude integrations, and SOC 2 Type II, GDPR, and HIPAA compliance with SSO and SCIM. Backchannel has no language matrix, no CRM sync, no compliance certifications, and asks you to run Docker and supply your own API key.
